# Electronics & computing

Phones, laptops, PC parts, peripherals, networking, wearables, cameras, audio.

## Retailers
- **Star Tech, Ryans, Techland** — PC parts, laptops, monitors, peripherals, networking. Static HTML; plain fetch usually works. They show a cash/special price and a higher regular price: record both.
- **Pickaboo, Apple Gadgets, Gadget & Gear** — phones, tablets, accessories. These carry both official and unofficial units; read each listing's labelling.
- **Brand stores** (Xiaomi, Samsung, Realme, Walton, etc.) — reference price for official units.
- **Daraz** — brand official stores and DarazMall only.

## Who "official" means
Official warranty runs through the brand's **BD distributor**, which differs per brand. Find the distributor on the brand's own BD site, then confirm the seller lists it as its source or is itself an authorized reseller. A shop calling a unit "official" is a claim, not evidence.

## Evidence
RTINGS, Notebookcheck, GSMArena tests, Hardware Unboxed, Gamers Nexus. Owner reports for long-term reliability (hinges, batteries, boards, panels).

## Variants to match exactly
RAM/storage, CPU generation (i5-1335U ≠ i5-1235U), regional chip (Exynos vs. Snapdragon), global vs. China ROM, panel type, included charger.

## Timing
Check for an announced or credibly leaked successor in the next ~3 months. A big drop on an older model often means stock clearance, which is fine if the user knows.

## Warranty
Official/brand vs. shop warranty vs. grey. Treat grey as no warranty. Note who services it and where.

## Phones and cellular devices
- Official vs. unofficial unit. Unofficial phones carry NEIR registration risk and may be blocked from local networks.
- Verify IMEI status through BTRC's NEIR service before buying a used or unofficial unit. Look up the current check method; don't rely on a remembered code.
- The official/unofficial price gap is usually the cost of that risk, not free savings.

## Refurbished, open-box, used
Common, and often sold without the label. If the price is far below other listings, or the listing says "box-opened", "like new", "imported used" or "renewed", treat it as refurbished. Check battery health (phones, laptops), IMEI (phones), and whether any warranty applies.

## PC builds
When the user is buying parts for one build, check compatibility before pricing: CPU socket and chipset, RAM type and speed, PSU wattage with headroom, GPU length and cooler height vs. case clearance, and motherboard form factor vs. case. List any part you couldn't confirm.

## Import notes
Anything with a radio (Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, cellular) may need BTRC approval to clear customs.

# Supplements: protein, vitamins, sports & health

High fake rate, and health-adjacent. Treat it as a mix of beauty (authenticity) and medicines (boundary).

## Retailers
- Distributors the **brand itself** names for Bangladesh.
- Brand official stores on Daraz.
- Licensed pharmacies (see `medicines.md`) for vitamins and health supplements.
- FB pages and non-mall Daraz sellers: assume fake or grey until proven otherwise. Whey protein in particular is widely counterfeited.

## Authenticity
Same table as `beauty.md`: scratch codes, seals, holograms and importer stickers are red-flag screens, not proof. A big discount on a premium brand is a red flag. Check whether the product has a local importer label and registration with the relevant BD regulator; look up which regulator and what the label should show, since this has changed over time.

## Evidence
- Third-party testing (Informed Sport, NSF Certified for Sport, Labdoor, independent lab tests) beats brand claims.
- For protein: check protein per serving vs. scoop size, and whether amino-acid spiking is flagged in independent tests.

## Price
Compare **৳ per serving** and, for protein, **৳ per 25 g of protein**. Tub price alone is meaningless.

## Check
- Expiry and storage (heat damages some products; grey stock may have sat in hot warehouses).
- Exact variant: flavour, size, formulation (isolate vs. concentrate, vitamin form and dose).

## Medical boundary
- Don't recommend supplements to treat or prevent a condition, or advise on doses beyond what the label states.
- If the user mentions a medical condition, pregnancy, medications they take, or a child as the user of the product, recommend checking with a doctor or pharmacist first.
- Weight-loss, "fat burner", testosterone-boosting and similar products: flag that they are a common source of undeclared drug ingredients, and don't help optimise them.

## Import notes
Supplements can be held at customs or need permits. Flag before recommending an import.
